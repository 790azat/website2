American Express, SoFi, and Discover Personal Loans Compared

Personal loans can provide a fixed amount of financing for debt consolidation, home improvements, major purchases, or other qualifying expenses. But the terms available to borrowers can vary considerably between lenders, particularly when it comes to loan amounts, annual percentage rates (APRs), origination fees, repayment periods, eligibility requirements, and funding speed.

American Express, SoFi, and Discover each offer personal loans with different structures. American Express Personal Loans are available only to eligible existing American Express Card Members who receive an offer, while SoFi and Discover allow prospective borrowers to check their potential terms through their respective application processes.

Because actual loan offers depend on individual creditworthiness and other factors, comparing the published product structures is more useful than assuming one lender will provide the same terms to every borrower.

American Express Personal Loans

American Express Personal Loans are unsecured installment loans issued by American Express National Bank. They are distinct from American Express credit cards and do not earn Membership Rewards points or other card rewards.

One of the most important eligibility considerations is that borrowers must receive an offer to apply and have an active eligible American Express Consumer Card. Applicants must also meet the lender's other requirements, including U.S. citizenship or qualifying residency.

Loan amounts start at $3,500, with the maximum amount determined by the borrower's eligibility and offer. American Express states that personal loans can be used for purposes such as debt consolidation, home repairs, remodeling, and major personal expenses. However, the loans cannot be used for real estate, business purposes, post-secondary education expenses, securities, or vehicle purchases other than a vehicle down payment.

American Express does not charge application or origination fees and does not impose a prepayment penalty. Its current terms state that late or returned payments can result in a $39 fee.

Another feature is the relatively direct funding process. After signing the loan agreement and completing required verification, American Express says funds are typically sent to the designated bank account in one day, although the recipient's bank may take additional time to post the funds.

SoFi Personal Loans

SoFi offers personal loans with amounts ranging from $5,000 to $100,000, according to its current published information. The loans carry fixed rates and can be used for lawful personal, family, or household purposes, subject to restrictions. Post-secondary education expenses are excluded.

As of September 21, 2026, SoFi's published fixed APR range is 6.49% to 35.49%, including its stated 0.25% autopay discount and 0.25% member rate discount. The available rate depends on factors including creditworthiness, income, loan term, and other underwriting considerations.

SoFi offers repayment terms ranging from two to seven years on its published rate table. Longer repayment periods can reduce the required monthly payment but may increase the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

A significant distinction is SoFi's origination-fee structure. Its current disclosures state that origination fees can range from 0% to 7%, depending on the selected loan option. When an origination fee applies, it is deducted from the loan proceeds.

For example, a borrower requesting $20,000 may receive less than $20,000 in cash if the selected loan includes an origination fee. The borrower should therefore distinguish between the loan amount and the net amount actually received.

SoFi allows prospective borrowers to check rates and terms through a soft credit inquiry. If the borrower proceeds with the application, SoFi says it will request a full credit report, which is considered a hard inquiry.

Discover Personal Loans

Discover Personal Loans provide another fixed-rate installment-loan option.

Discover states that borrowers can check their rate without affecting their credit score through a soft credit inquiry. The eventual interest rate depends on factors such as credit history, application information, and selected loan term.

Discover's published personal-loan terms cover repayment periods from 36 to 84 months, or three to seven years.

A notable feature is its fee structure. Discover states that its personal loans have no origination fees, and its current consumer information also states that there are no closing fees or prepayment penalties.

The company also emphasizes that personal loans have fixed rates, meaning the scheduled payment remains consistent as long as the borrower makes payments according to the loan agreement.

Discover does not require collateral for its personal loans. However, approval and the rate offered depend on the borrower's creditworthiness and other application factors.

The Importance of APR

When comparing personal loans, the interest rate is only one part of the cost.

The annual percentage rate (APR) incorporates the interest cost and, where applicable, certain finance charges. This is particularly important when comparing a loan with an origination fee against one without one.

Consider a hypothetical $20,000 loan:

  • Lender A charges 10% interest with no origination fee.
  • Lender B charges 9.5% interest but deducts a 5% origination fee.

The second loan has a lower stated interest rate, but the borrower receives only $19,000 before considering other applicable costs.

That does not automatically make either loan more expensive. The result depends on the repayment period and other terms. It illustrates why borrowers should compare APR, total interest, fees, monthly payment, and net proceeds together.

Discover's current educational material similarly notes that a lower interest rate does not necessarily produce a lower total borrowing cost when fees are involved.

Loan Term and Monthly Payment

The repayment period can significantly affect the total cost of borrowing.

Suppose a borrower takes a fixed-rate personal loan. Choosing a longer term generally spreads repayment over more months, which can lower the required monthly payment.

The tradeoff is that interest can accumulate for a longer period.

For example, a borrower might prefer a seven-year term because the payment fits comfortably within the household budget. Another borrower may choose a three-year term because they can handle the larger monthly payment and want to reduce the time interest accrues.

There is no universal term that works for every borrower.

The relevant comparison is between monthly affordability and total borrowing cost.

Which Expenses Can the Loans Cover?

Personal loans are commonly used for expenses such as:

  • Debt consolidation
  • Home improvements
  • Major purchases
  • Medical expenses
  • Weddings or other major life events
  • Unexpected household expenses

However, permitted uses vary by lender.

American Express, for example, specifically excludes real estate, business expenses, securities, post-secondary education, and certain vehicle purchases.

SoFi permits lawful personal, family, or household purposes but excludes post-secondary education expenses.

Borrowers should check the applicable loan agreement before assuming a particular expense is eligible.

Credit Checks and Prequalification

Checking potential loan terms before submitting a full application can help borrowers understand their options without immediately triggering a hard credit inquiry.

SoFi explicitly states that its initial rate check uses a soft credit pull, while continuing the application can involve a hard inquiry.

Discover likewise allows prospective borrowers to check their rate without an impact to their credit score from that initial check.

American Express uses a somewhat different model because its personal loan product is offered to eligible existing Card Members. Its terms state that the borrower must receive an offer to apply and continue to satisfy eligibility requirements.

Borrowers should therefore distinguish between checking an estimated offer and submitting a full loan application.

Funding Speed

The timing of funding can matter when a borrower has a time-sensitive expense.

American Express states that it typically sends funds within one day after the loan agreement is signed and required verification is completed, although bank posting times can vary.

SoFi and Discover also provide online application processes, but actual funding timing depends on approval, verification, loan documentation, and the borrower's bank.

Anyone borrowing for an urgent expense should verify the lender's current funding timeline rather than relying on a general expectation of same-day or next-day funding.

What Borrowers Should Compare

Rather than comparing lenders based on brand recognition or a single advertised rate, borrowers can build a simple comparison around the actual offers received.

Key figures include:

Loan amount: How much money will be borrowed?

Net proceeds: How much cash will actually reach the borrower's account after any fees?

APR: What is the effective annualized cost?

Monthly payment: Does the payment fit comfortably within the budget?

Total payments: How much will be repaid over the full term?

Origination fees: Is any portion of the loan withheld before funding?

Repayment period: How quickly must the balance be repaid?

Prepayment rules: Can the loan be paid off early without an additional charge?

Late-payment costs: What happens if a payment is missed or returned?

Permitted use: Is the intended expense allowed under the lender's terms?

A Practical Example

Imagine a borrower needs $25,000 to consolidate several higher-interest debts.

The borrower receives three offers:

  • American Express: fixed-rate loan with no origination fee
  • SoFi: fixed-rate loan with either a no-fee option or an option involving an origination fee
  • Discover: fixed-rate loan with no origination fee

The borrower should not automatically choose the offer with the lowest advertised interest rate.

Instead, the borrower can calculate:

Amount received − fees = usable proceeds

and then compare:

Monthly payment + total interest + applicable fees = overall borrowing cost

If the borrower needs exactly $25,000 to pay creditors, an origination fee could mean requesting a larger loan to receive the desired net amount. That can increase the balance and potentially increase interest costs.

Questions to Ask Before Accepting a Personal Loan

Before signing, borrowers should confirm:

  1. What is my exact APR?
  2. What is the interest rate?
  3. Is there an origination fee?
  4. How much cash will I actually receive?
  5. What is my monthly payment?
  6. How much will I repay in total?
  7. What repayment terms are available?
  8. Is the rate fixed for the entire loan?
  9. Are there late-payment or returned-payment fees?
  10. Can I pay the loan off early without a penalty?
  11. Is my intended use permitted?
  12. Will continuing the application result in a hard credit inquiry?
  13. How quickly will the funds be deposited?
  14. Could any condition change before funding?

The loan agreement should be reviewed carefully because the final terms—not the initial advertising or rate estimate—control the borrowing relationship.

Comparing the Three Lenders in Context

American Express, SoFi, and Discover approach personal lending differently.

American Express has a particularly restrictive entry point because its personal loans are available to eligible existing Card Members who receive an offer. It also does not charge an origination fee and provides fixed monthly payments.

SoFi offers a broader loan range, with amounts up to $100,000 and terms extending to seven years. Its current pricing structure includes options with origination fees, making net proceeds and APR particularly important when comparing offers.

Discover offers fixed-rate personal loans and does not charge origination fees, while allowing prospective borrowers to check their rate without an initial credit-score impact.

The actual cost for any borrower depends on credit history, income, loan amount, term, state-specific rules, and the lender's underwriting model.

For that reason, the most useful comparison is ultimately between actual personalized loan offers, with attention to APR, fees, monthly payments, total repayment, and net funds received.
