Wire Transfers: How Domestic and International Transfers Work

Wire transfers are one of the most widely used ways to move money electronically between bank accounts. Individuals use them for large purchases, real estate transactions, tuition payments, family support, business payments, and international transfers. Businesses also rely on wires when payments need to reach another financial institution with a high degree of speed and certainty.

A wire transfer can be domestic, involving accounts within the same country, or international, involving financial institutions in different countries. The process, fees, required information, exchange rates, processing times, and consumer protections can differ significantly between the two.

Understanding how wire transfers work can help you avoid unnecessary fees, incorrect account information, delays, and unexpected currency-conversion costs.

What Is a Wire Transfer?

A wire transfer is an electronic movement of funds from one bank or financial institution to another. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) describes wire transfers as a common method of electronically moving money between bank accounts, including both domestic and international transfers.

Unlike an ACH transfer, which moves through the Automated Clearing House network, a bank wire generally involves a financial institution sending a payment instruction through a wire-transfer network or correspondent banking system.

In the United States, one major domestic infrastructure is the Federal Reserve's Fedwire Funds Service. Fedwire is a real-time gross settlement system used by financial institutions for high-value and time-sensitive payments. Payments accepted through Fedwire are settled individually and are final and irrevocable under the service's rules.

For consumers, however, the term "wire transfer" can encompass several different mechanisms depending on the financial institution and destination.

How a Domestic Wire Transfer Works

A domestic wire typically moves money between two financial institutions within the same country.

For example, a buyer purchasing a home may instruct their bank to send funds to a title company's account. A business may wire money to a supplier, or an individual may transfer funds between accounts held at different banks.

The process generally involves:

  1. The sender provides payment instructions.
  2. The sending institution verifies the transaction and account information.
  3. The payment is transmitted through an appropriate wire network.
  4. The receiving financial institution receives the payment instruction.
  5. The recipient's account is credited according to the receiving institution's procedures.

The exact process varies by bank and transfer type.

Domestic wires are often used when the payment needs to be available more quickly than a standard bank transfer or when the recipient specifically requires a wire.

What Information Is Needed for a Wire?

Banks generally require detailed information before initiating a wire. The exact requirements depend on the destination and financial institutions involved.

For a domestic U.S. wire, information may include:

  • Recipient's full name
  • Recipient's bank name
  • Recipient's account number
  • Routing number
  • Recipient address in some circumstances
  • Transfer amount
  • Purpose or reference information when required

International wires typically require additional information, such as:

  • Recipient's full legal name
  • Recipient's address
  • Recipient bank name and address
  • Account number or IBAN
  • SWIFT/BIC code where applicable
  • Currency of the payment
  • Intermediary bank information when required
  • Payment purpose or other compliance information

The sender should obtain wire instructions directly from the intended recipient or institution using a trusted communication channel.

A fraudulent change to wiring instructions can redirect funds to an account controlled by someone else. This is particularly important for real estate closings, business payments, and large invoices.

Domestic Wire Transfer Fees

The cost of a domestic wire depends on the financial institution and account type.

A bank may charge separately for:

  • Outgoing domestic wires
  • Incoming domestic wires
  • Same-day processing
  • Branch-initiated transfers
  • Online wire transfers
  • Expedited services

A bank's fee schedule may distinguish between consumer and business accounts. Some premium or business accounts may include reduced wire fees, while other accounts charge for every outgoing transaction.

It is also important to distinguish the fee charged to the customer from the underlying cost of the financial network. For example, the Federal Reserve's 2026 Fedwire pricing schedule charges participating financial institutions based on transaction volume; those institutional fees are not the same thing as the retail wire fee a consumer sees on a bank statement.

When comparing bank accounts, looking only at monthly maintenance fees can therefore miss an important cost for customers who frequently send wires.

How International Wire Transfers Work

An international wire moves funds between countries and may involve multiple financial institutions.

A simplified international payment can look like this:

Sender → Sending Bank → Correspondent/Intermediary Bank → Receiving Bank → Recipient

Not every transfer uses an intermediary bank, but correspondent institutions can be involved when the sending and receiving banks do not have a direct relationship.

The transfer may also require currency conversion. For example, a sender could fund a transfer in U.S. dollars while the recipient receives euros, pounds, Canadian dollars, or another currency.

This creates two separate cost considerations:

Transfer fees are charges associated with sending or receiving the payment.

Foreign-exchange costs arise when one currency is converted into another.

The exchange rate can materially affect the final amount received. CFPB guidance requires covered remittance-transfer providers to disclose applicable exchange-rate information and certain fees and taxes for qualifying transactions.

International Wire Transfer Fees and Exchange Rates

An international transfer can have more than one cost.

Potential charges include:

  • Outgoing wire fees
  • Currency-conversion costs
  • Correspondent-bank fees
  • Receiving-bank fees
  • Foreign taxes
  • Additional service charges

A transfer advertised as having a low upfront fee can still result in a different amount reaching the recipient because of the exchange rate or other charges.

For qualifying U.S. consumer remittance transfers, providers generally must disclose the amount being sent, applicable fees and taxes, exchange-rate information when relevant, and the amount expected to reach the recipient.

This makes the total amount received more useful for comparing international transfer options than the advertised transfer fee alone.

How Long Do Wire Transfers Take?

Wire-transfer timing depends on the sending bank, receiving bank, destination country, currency, cutoff time, compliance reviews, weekends, holidays, and whether an intermediary bank is involved.

A domestic wire may be processed the same business day if it is submitted before the bank's cutoff time and meets its requirements. A transfer submitted after the cutoff may be processed on the following business day.

International transfers can take longer because they may pass through several financial institutions and may require additional screening or currency conversion.

For time-sensitive payments, consumers should ask the sending institution:

  • What is the wire cutoff time?
  • When will the transfer leave the account?
  • When should the recipient expect funds?
  • Could an intermediary bank delay the payment?
  • Are additional fees possible?
  • Is the payment being sent in the recipient's local currency?

Wire Transfers vs. ACH Transfers

Wire transfers and ACH transfers are both electronic, but they serve different purposes.

Feature Wire Transfer ACH Transfer
Typical use Large or time-sensitive payments Payroll, bills, recurring transfers
Processing Often faster Can take longer depending on transaction type
Fees Often charged Often free or lower cost
Reversal Can be difficult once processed Rules differ by transaction
Information required Detailed bank instructions Routing and account information
International use Common Depends on provider and network
Common business use Real estate, suppliers, high-value payments Payroll, invoices, recurring payments

Neither method is universally appropriate. The choice depends on speed, cost, payment size, recipient requirements, and the type of transaction.

Are Wire Transfers Reversible?

One of the most important characteristics of a wire is that recovering incorrectly sent funds can be difficult.

If you enter the wrong account information, send money to a fraudulent recipient, or approve a payment after being deceived, contact the financial institution immediately.

Do not wait until the transaction appears on your monthly statement.

For certain U.S. international remittance transfers covered by federal rules, consumers generally have cancellation rights within a specified period, subject to exceptions such as the recipient already receiving or collecting the money.

The protections available for a particular transaction depend on the type of transfer, provider, destination, and applicable law.

Wire Transfer Fraud and Security

Wire fraud is a significant concern because wires can move large amounts of money quickly.

Common warning signs include:

  • Last-minute changes to payment instructions
  • Requests to send funds to a different bank account
  • Urgent demands to bypass normal verification
  • Unexpected instructions received by email
  • Requests to keep the transaction secret
  • Payment instructions that differ from previously verified information

For large transactions, independently verify the recipient's wire instructions using a known telephone number or another trusted communication method.

For real estate transactions, for example, a buyer should not rely solely on an email containing last-minute wiring instructions.

International Wire Transfers and Compliance Checks

International payments may be subject to financial-institution compliance procedures. Banks can request additional information about the sender, recipient, purpose of payment, source of funds, or destination.

A legitimate transfer can therefore be delayed while a financial institution reviews the transaction.

Providing accurate information at the beginning can reduce avoidable delays. Attempting to disguise the purpose of a payment or providing inconsistent information can create additional problems.

How to Compare Wire Transfer Costs

When comparing banks or money-transfer providers, consider the entire transaction rather than the advertised wire fee.

For a domestic transfer, examine:

  • Outgoing wire fee
  • Incoming wire fee
  • Online versus branch pricing
  • Cutoff times
  • Same-day availability
  • Account requirements

For an international transfer, also examine:

  • Exchange rate
  • Currency-conversion spread
  • Correspondent-bank fees
  • Recipient-bank fees
  • Foreign taxes
  • Expected amount received
  • Transfer availability date

A provider charging a slightly higher transfer fee may still result in a lower overall cost if its exchange rate and other charges are more favorable.

Questions to Ask Before Sending a Wire

Before authorizing a large domestic or international transfer, consider asking:

  1. What is the total cost of the transfer?
  2. What exchange rate will apply?
  3. How much will the recipient actually receive?
  4. When will the funds become available?
  5. What information does the recipient need?
  6. Are intermediary-bank charges possible?
  7. What is the bank's wire cutoff time?
  8. Can the transfer be canceled?
  9. What should I do if the recipient does not receive the funds?
  10. How should I independently verify the recipient's wiring instructions?

The Bottom Line

Wire transfers provide a direct way to move money electronically between financial institutions, making them useful for large payments, business transactions, real estate purchases, and international transfers.

Domestic wires generally involve fewer institutions and no currency conversion, while international wires can involve correspondent banks, foreign-exchange costs, additional fees, and more complicated processing.

The most important factors are not simply the advertised wire fee. Before sending money, consider the total cost, exchange rate, expected delivery time, recipient information, and ability to correct an error.

For international transfers from the United States that qualify as remittance transfers, federal rules can provide additional disclosures and consumer protections, including information about fees, exchange rates, the amount delivered, cancellation rights, and error-resolution procedures.
