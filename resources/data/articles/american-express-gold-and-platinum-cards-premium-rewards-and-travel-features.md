American Express offers two well-known premium rewards cards in its U.S. consumer lineup: the American Express® Gold Card and The Platinum Card® from American Express. Both earn Membership Rewards® points, but they are designed around different spending and travel patterns.

The Gold Card carries a \$325 annual fee and places considerable emphasis on dining, groceries, and everyday rewards, while also providing selected travel and lifestyle credits. The Platinum Card carries an \$895 annual fee and focuses more heavily on airport lounge access, hotels, travel credits, and premium lifestyle benefits.

Because both cards use Membership Rewards points, consumers may compare them based on the earning structure, annual fees, credits, and travel benefits rather than simply looking at the number of perks attached to each account.

## American Express Gold Card: Everyday Rewards With Travel Features

The Gold Card has a \$325 annual fee and is structured around spending categories that many households use regularly.

Cardmembers currently earn:

* 4X Membership Rewards points at restaurants worldwide, including eligible takeout and delivery in the U.S., on up to \$50,000 in purchases per calendar year
* 4X points at U.S. supermarkets, on up to \$25,000 per calendar year
* 5X points on prepaid hotels booked through AmexTravel.com or the Amex Travel App
* 3X points on flights booked directly with airlines or through AmexTravel.com or the Amex Travel App
* 2X points on eligible prepaid car rentals and cruises booked through Amex Travel
* 1X point on other eligible purchases

The spending caps on the restaurant and supermarket categories are important. Once the applicable annual limit is reached, additional eligible spending generally earns at the standard rate rather than the elevated 4X rate.

For consumers with substantial restaurant and grocery spending, the Gold Card's earning structure can generate a large portion of its rewards through everyday purchases rather than travel bookings.

## Gold Card Dining and Lifestyle Credits

The Gold Card's benefits extend beyond Membership Rewards points.

Current benefits include up to \$120 annually in Uber Cash, provided the card is added to an eligible Uber account and the applicable requirements are met. Cardmembers can receive \$10 in Uber Cash each month for eligible Uber rides and Uber Eats orders in the U.S. when they select an Amex Card for the transaction. Enrollment is required.

The card also provides a \$120 annual dining credit through participating businesses, along with an \$84 annual Dunkin' credit. These benefits require enrollment and have specific monthly or merchant restrictions.

The Gold Card also offers up to \$100 in annual Resy statement credits, with eligible purchases at participating U.S. Resy restaurants qualifying subject to the benefit terms.

These credits can reduce the effective cost of the \$325 annual fee for someone who already uses the participating services. However, a credit should not automatically be treated as cash value. If a cardholder would not otherwise spend money with an eligible merchant, the nominal credit amount may not represent an equivalent financial benefit.

## Gold Card Travel Benefits

The Gold Card is not primarily an airport-lounge card.

American Express specifically notes that the Gold Card does not include airport lounge access. Instead, its travel benefits include rewards on flights and hotels, The Hotel Collection benefits, Uber Cash, and other travel-related features.

Through The Hotel Collection, eligible Gold Cardmembers can receive benefits when booking qualifying properties through Amex Travel. A two-night minimum stay is required, and benefits can include a \$100 hotel credit toward eligible charges, noon check-in, late 4 p.m. checkout, and room upgrades when available.

The Gold Card can therefore function as a hybrid card: its strongest everyday earning categories are dining and groceries, while travel benefits provide additional value for people who also take trips.

## Platinum Card: A Much Larger Travel and Lifestyle Package

The Platinum Card takes a different approach.

The current U.S. consumer Platinum Card has an \$895 annual fee. American Express says the card provides more than \$3,500 in potential annual value across travel, dining, entertainment, and other benefits, although the actual value a cardmember receives depends on eligibility, enrollment, spending, and ability to use the credits.

The Platinum Card's earning structure includes:

* 5X Membership Rewards points on eligible flights booked directly with airlines or through Amex Travel, on up to \$500,000 in purchases per calendar year
* 5X points on prepaid hotels booked through Amex Travel
* 1X point on other eligible purchases

This makes the Platinum Card considerably more travel-focused than Gold when it comes to its headline rewards categories.

## Platinum Card Airport Lounge Access

One of the clearest differences between the two cards is airport lounge access.

The Platinum Card includes access to the American Express Global Lounge Collection, which American Express currently describes as providing access to more than 1,550 airport lounges across more than 500 airports worldwide. Enrollment may be required for some programs.

The collection includes Centurion Lounges, Priority Pass Select lounges, and other participating lounge networks. Eligible Platinum Cardmembers can also receive access to Delta Sky Club locations when flying an eligible Delta flight, subject to the applicable rules and visit limitations.

For frequent travelers, lounge access can be one of the most visible differences between Platinum and Gold.

The Gold Card does not provide this same lounge-access package.

## Platinum Hotel Credit

The Platinum Card includes a \$600 annual hotel credit.

Cardmembers can receive up to \$300 in statement credits during each half of the year on eligible prepaid Fine Hotels \+ Resorts® or The Hotel Collection bookings made through Amex Travel with the Platinum Card. The Hotel Collection requires a minimum two-night stay.

This is a calendar-year benefit divided into two periods rather than one unrestricted \$600 credit that can be used at any point during the year.

The booking requirements also matter. A hotel reservation made outside the qualifying Amex Travel channels generally will not qualify for the credit.

## Platinum Uber Cash

The Platinum Card provides up to \$200 in Uber Cash annually when the card is added to an eligible Uber account.

American Express currently describes the benefit as \$15 in Uber Cash each month, plus an additional \$20 in December, for up to \$200 annually. The benefit can be used for eligible Uber rides and Uber Eats purchases in the U.S., subject to the applicable requirements.

The Platinum Card also currently offers up to \$120 annually toward an auto-renewing Uber One membership when the membership is purchased with the card, subject to the terms.

These benefits can be useful for consumers who already use Uber regularly, but their practical value depends on actual usage.

## Platinum Resy and Dining Benefits

The Platinum Card also places significant emphasis on dining.

Cardmembers can receive up to \$400 in annual Resy statement credits, structured as up to \$100 per quarter on eligible purchases at participating U.S. Resy restaurants. Enrollment is required.

The benefit is therefore different from simply earning additional points at restaurants. The value comes through statement credits, while the Gold Card combines a strong restaurant earning rate with its own dining-related credits.

Consumers who frequently dine at participating Resy restaurants may find the Platinum benefit easier to use than someone who rarely visits participating establishments.

## Global Entry and TSA PreCheck Credits

Both cards can provide credits related to trusted-traveler programs, although the current Platinum benefit is particularly useful for frequent travelers.

The Platinum Card offers either a \$120 statement credit for a Global Entry application fee or up to an \$85 credit for an eligible TSA PreCheck application fee. The Global Entry credit is available every four years, while the TSA PreCheck credit can apply every 4.5 years for a five-year membership when the applicable fee is charged to the Platinum Card.

The Gold Card also offers travel-related benefits, but the Platinum package is substantially broader.

## Fine Hotels \+ Resorts

Platinum Cardmembers have access to American Express Fine Hotels \+ Resorts, a collection of participating premium properties booked through Amex Travel.

Eligible reservations can include benefits such as daily breakfast for two, a property credit, room upgrades when available, and guaranteed 4 p.m. late checkout, depending on the property's participation and applicable terms.

The program is particularly relevant to travelers who prefer higher-end hotels and can make use of the benefits attached to eligible reservations.

The Platinum Card's hotel strategy therefore differs from the Gold Card's. Gold offers a smaller selection of travel benefits alongside strong everyday earning, while Platinum incorporates hotels more deeply into its overall benefits structure.

## Membership Rewards Points and Transfer Partners

Both cards earn Membership Rewards points, allowing cardmembers to participate in the same broader rewards ecosystem.

Membership Rewards points can be used for travel, transferred to participating airline and hotel loyalty programs, redeemed for certain gift cards or eligible purchases, and used through other redemption options offered by American Express.

The ability to transfer points can be particularly important for travelers because the value and usefulness of Membership Rewards can vary depending on the loyalty program selected and the redemption being made.

Consumers considering either card should therefore evaluate not just how many points they can earn, but how they intend to redeem them.

## Gold vs. Platinum: Different Spending Profiles

The two cards can serve substantially different purposes.

The Gold Card centers its earning power on dining and U.S. supermarket purchases. Its \$325 annual fee is paired with dining, Uber, Resy, Dunkin', hotel, and other benefits.

The Platinum Card has an \$895 annual fee and puts greater emphasis on premium travel. Its benefits include extensive lounge access, hotel credits, Uber Cash, Resy credits, Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credits, Fine Hotels \+ Resorts, and other lifestyle benefits.

The difference is therefore not simply about which card offers more rewards. Gold directs more of its rewards structure toward everyday food spending, while Platinum places substantially more of its annual fee toward travel and lifestyle benefits.

## Considering the Annual Fee

The headline annual fees are only the starting point.

For Gold, a cardholder could calculate how much value they can realistically receive from restaurant rewards, supermarket rewards, Uber Cash, dining credits, Resy credits, Dunkin' credits, and travel benefits.

For Platinum, the calculation involves a much longer list of benefits, including the hotel credit, lounge access, Uber Cash, Uber One credit, Resy credit, Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credit, Fine Hotels \+ Resorts benefits, and other available statement credits.

The important distinction is between a benefit's advertised value and the value it has to a particular cardholder. A \$100 statement credit does not necessarily save \$100 if the cardholder would not otherwise make the qualifying purchase.

## Which Card Fits Which Type of User?

The Gold Card's structure is oriented toward consumers who spend heavily on dining and groceries and can also use its lifestyle and travel credits.

The Platinum Card is structured for consumers who place greater emphasis on premium travel, airport lounges, hotels, and a broad collection of statement credits and travel-related services.

Someone who rarely flies may have less opportunity to use Platinum's lounge and hotel benefits. Conversely, someone who spends heavily on restaurants and U.S. supermarkets may find Gold's category-based earning structure more directly aligned with everyday purchases.

Neither card's annual fee should be evaluated independently of actual spending and travel behavior. The relevant comparison is between the benefits available under the current terms and the expenses the cardholder would realistically make without the card.

## Reviewing the Current Terms Before Applying

American Express regularly changes credit-card benefits, annual fees, rewards structures, and introductory offers. The 2025 Platinum refresh, for example, introduced or expanded several credits while keeping the card's premium positioning, and the current 2026 benefits reflect that newer structure.

Before applying, consumers should review the current American Express application page, rates and fees, benefit terms, spending caps, enrollment requirements, and eligibility language.

For Gold, particular attention should go to the annual fee, restaurant and supermarket spending limits, and the conditions attached to the dining and lifestyle credits.

For Platinum, the larger annual fee makes it especially important to examine which credits and travel benefits can realistically be used throughout the year.

Both cards offer Membership Rewards points and premium-oriented benefits, but their structures are noticeably different. Gold combines strong everyday earning with selected travel and lifestyle features, while Platinum is built around a much broader premium travel and benefits package.
