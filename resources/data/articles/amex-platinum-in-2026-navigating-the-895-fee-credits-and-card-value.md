The **American Express Platinum Card®** has evolved into something considerably broader than a traditional travel-rewards credit card. In 2026, its \$895 annual fee is supported by a large collection of travel, dining, entertainment, shopping, and lifestyle credits.

That creates both the card's appeal and its biggest complication.

American Express currently advertises more than **\$3,500 in potential annual value** across its Platinum benefits. But much of that value comes through individual statement credits with specific merchants, enrollment requirements, booking restrictions, quarterly or semiannual deadlines, and other conditions.

For a cardholder who naturally uses many of these services, the credits can substantially offset the fee. For someone who has to manufacture spending simply to use the benefits, the advertised value can look considerably better on paper than it feels in practice.

## The \$895 Annual Fee Changes the Calculation

The Platinum Card's current annual fee is **\$895**. Additional Platinum Cards cost \$195 each, although American Express also offers a no-fee Companion Platinum Card with a more limited set of benefits.

That is a significant recurring expense, so evaluating the card requires looking beyond the welcome offer.

The relevant question is:

**How much of the card's ongoing benefits would you actually use without changing your normal spending habits?**

For example, a traveler who already books eligible luxury hotels, uses Uber regularly, subscribes to qualifying entertainment services, visits airports with participating lounges, and dines at eligible Resy restaurants may naturally use several credits.

Another cardholder might rarely use Uber, have no interest in Equinox or lululemon, rarely book through Amex Travel, and have little reason to use certain entertainment credits.

Both people would pay the same \$895 annual fee.

## The \$600 Hotel Credit

The hotel credit is one of the largest recurring benefits.

Platinum Card Members can receive up to **\$300 in statement credits from January through June and another \$300 from July through December**, for a maximum of \$600 per calendar year. The purchases must generally be prepaid eligible Fine Hotels \+ Resorts or The Hotel Collection bookings made through American Express Travel. The Hotel Collection requires a minimum two-night stay.

This structure is important because the credit is not a general hotel rebate.

Someone who regularly books qualifying properties through Amex Travel may find the credit relatively easy to use. A traveler who normally books inexpensive independent hotels directly with the property or through another booking platform may have difficulty extracting the full amount.

The value also depends on hotel pricing. Booking a more expensive property solely to consume the credit does not necessarily create a financial gain.

## The \$400 Resy Credit

The Platinum Card now offers up to **\$100 per quarter**, or \$400 annually, for eligible Resy purchases.

The credit applies to qualifying purchases at eligible U.S. restaurants and other food-and-beverage establishments that participate in the Resy ecosystem. Enrollment is required. As of September 15, 2026, qualifying purchases can also include certain eligible transactions through Tock.

For someone who already spends \$100 or more each quarter at participating restaurants, the credit can be relatively straightforward.

But the quarterly structure creates a potential expiration problem. A cardholder who spends nothing eligible during one quarter generally cannot carry that unused \$100 into the next period.

That makes the practical value dependent on both spending and timing.

## Entertainment Credits Require Attention

The Platinum's digital-entertainment credit provides up to **\$25 per month**, or \$300 annually, toward eligible services.

Participating services currently include options such as Disney+, ESPN streaming services, Hulu, The New York Times, Paramount+, Peacock, The Wall Street Journal, YouTube Premium, and YouTube TV, subject to the applicable terms.

This can be relatively easy to use for households that already subscribe to several qualifying services.

However, it is another example of why the advertised annual value should not be interpreted as guaranteed savings. Paying for a subscription you would not otherwise want simply to activate a credit creates an expense rather than a saving.

## Airport Lounge Access Remains a Major Feature

The Platinum Card provides access to the **American Express Global Lounge Collection**, which currently covers more than 1,550 lounges across more than 500 airports, according to American Express.

The network includes Centurion Lounges, Priority Pass locations after enrollment, and other participating partner lounges. Platinum Card Members can also receive 10 Delta Sky Club visits per year when flying on eligible Delta flights under the applicable rules. Cardholders who spend \$75,000 in eligible purchases during a calendar year can unlock unlimited Delta Sky Club access for the remainder of that year and the following year.

For frequent international travelers, lounge access can represent a substantial convenience.

Its financial value, however, depends on actual airport usage. Someone taking only a few trips per year may place far less value on lounge access than someone who regularly makes long international journeys.

## The \$200 Airline Fee Credit

The Platinum Card also provides up to **\$200 per calendar year** in statement credits for eligible incidental fees charged by one qualifying airline selected by the cardholder.

Eligible expenses can include certain baggage fees and other qualifying incidental charges. Enrollment and airline selection are required, and American Express notes that qualifying airlines and eligible transactions are subject to the applicable terms.

This is another credit that should be valued according to actual travel behavior.

If you routinely pay eligible baggage or other incidental charges with the selected airline, the credit can reduce an expense you already incur.

If you rarely encounter qualifying fees, the nominal \$200 figure has less practical significance.

## The Platinum Card Is More About Benefits Than Everyday Spending

One important consideration is the card's ordinary earning structure.

The Platinum Card earns **5 Membership Rewards points per dollar on eligible flights purchased directly from airlines or through American Express Travel, up to \$500,000 in purchases per calendar year, and 5 points per dollar on prepaid hotels booked through Amex Travel**. Other purchases generally earn 1 point per dollar unless another applicable bonus category or offer applies.

That means the Platinum isn't necessarily the card a household would use for every expense.

Someone who spends heavily on restaurants, groceries, or other everyday categories may use another rewards card for those purchases while keeping Platinum primarily for airfare, hotels, lounge access, credits, and premium travel benefits.

The value proposition therefore depends on the broader card strategy rather than the Platinum operating in isolation.

## Hotel Status Adds Another Layer of Value

The Platinum Card includes complimentary elite-status opportunities with participating hotel programs after enrollment.

The current benefits include **Hilton Honors Gold** and **Marriott Bonvoy Gold Elite** status, subject to enrollment and program terms. American Express also added Leaders Club Sterling Status from The Leading Hotels of the World as part of the Platinum refresh.

Hotel status can provide benefits such as upgrades when available, late checkout, and other property-specific privileges.

Again, the practical value depends on where a cardholder stays. Someone who rarely uses participating hotel programs may receive little value from the status.

## Calculating Your Personal Break-Even Point

A useful way to evaluate the \$895 fee is to ignore the advertised total and create a personal value worksheet.

For example:

**Hotel credit:** \$400 of realistic value
**Resy:** \$250
**Uber:** \$150
**Digital entertainment:** \$180
**Airline fee credit:** \$150
**Lounge access:** \$200
**Other benefits:** \$100

That hypothetical cardholder would assign approximately \$1,430 of practical annual value to the card.

Subtract the \$895 annual fee:

**\$1,430 − \$895 \= \$535 of estimated net value**

This is only an illustration. Another cardholder could reasonably value the same benefits at substantially less.

The important exercise is to use **real spending patterns rather than advertised maximums**.

## Don't Double-Count Credits

A common mistake in premium-card calculations is assigning full value to every benefit without asking whether the spending overlaps.

Suppose someone spends \$600 on eligible hotels only because they have a \$600 hotel credit. The credit has reduced the cost of those hotel purchases, but the underlying hotel expense still exists.

Likewise, a \$300 entertainment credit is not equivalent to \$300 in unrestricted cash if the cardholder would not otherwise purchase the qualifying subscriptions.

A more realistic calculation asks:

> “What would I have spent on these products and services without the Platinum Card?”

That number provides a much more useful estimate of the card's economic value.

## Additional Cardholders Add Another Cost

An Additional Platinum Card currently costs **\$195 per year**. American Express says Additional Card Members can receive access to benefits including the Global Lounge Collection and certain hotel-status benefits, while the primary cardholder remains responsible for account activity.

For households that travel together, the additional-card economics can therefore become important.

The no-fee Companion Platinum Card provides another option, but it does not provide all of the benefits available to a paid Additional Platinum Card Member.

Families should compare the incremental fee with the benefits the additional cardholder would actually receive.

## A Premium Card With a High Management Cost

The Platinum's large benefit package can be valuable, but it comes with a hidden cost: **attention**.

Several credits require enrollment. Others reset monthly, quarterly, or semiannually. Some require booking through Amex Travel. Others require selecting an airline or maintaining a qualifying subscription.

That means the card can reward organized cardholders while being less compelling for someone who dislikes tracking multiple benefits.

A person who has to constantly rearrange purchases to use the credits may experience less practical value than the spreadsheet suggests.

## The 2026 Platinum Value Equation

The Platinum Card's current structure makes sense to evaluate in four layers:

**1\. Travel:**
How frequently do you fly, use airport lounges, and book hotels?

**2\. Existing spending:**
Would you naturally use Uber, Resy, qualifying entertainment services, Saks, lululemon, or other participating merchants?

**3\. Rewards:**
How much value can you realistically extract from Membership Rewards points?

**4\. Administrative effort:**
Are you willing to enroll in benefits, track quarterly and semiannual credits, select an airline, and monitor eligibility requirements?

The \$895 fee is only the starting point.

American Express currently estimates more than \$3,500 in annual potential value, but that figure represents the aggregate value of numerous benefits rather than an amount every cardholder will automatically receive.

For a frequent traveler who naturally uses a substantial portion of the credits and lounge benefits, the economics can look very different from those of an occasional traveler who primarily wants a simple rewards card.

The most useful way to evaluate the Platinum Card in 2026 is therefore not to ask whether **\$895 is expensive** in isolation. Instead, calculate the value of the benefits you would genuinely use, subtract the annual fee, and then compare that result with alternative travel and rewards cards.

That approach turns a long list of premium perks into a much clearer personal cost-and-value calculation.
