Health insurance can represent one of the largest recurring expenses for freelancers, independent contractors, consultants, and business owners who do not receive employer-subsidized coverage. The federal tax code provides a potentially valuable deduction for qualifying self-employed individuals who pay premiums for eligible health insurance.

The **Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction** can allow eligible taxpayers to deduct qualifying premiums for medical, dental, vision, and certain long-term care insurance as an adjustment to income rather than as an itemized medical expense. The deduction is generally reported on **Schedule 1 of Form 1040**, subject to several important limitations.

Understanding who qualifies, which premiums count, and how business structure affects the deduction can help self-employed individuals organize their health insurance and tax records more effectively.

## Who Can Claim the Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction?

Eligibility depends on both the taxpayer's business activity and the circumstances surrounding their health coverage.

Generally, the rules can apply to:

* Sole proprietors with qualifying net profit
* Certain partners with net earnings from self-employment
* Individuals using an optional method to calculate net earnings from self-employment
* More-than-2% S corporation shareholders who receive wages from the corporation

The insurance plan must also be established, or treated as established, under the applicable trade or business.

### The Net-Earnings Limitation

The deduction generally cannot exceed the taxpayer's earned income from the trade or business under which the insurance plan is established.

For a Schedule C business, this generally means the deduction is limited by the relevant business's net profit after applicable adjustments.

This creates an important distinction: paying \$15,000 in qualifying premiums does not automatically mean a taxpayer can deduct the entire \$15,000 if the applicable earned income limitation is lower.

If a taxpayer has multiple businesses, the calculation may need to be performed separately for each business under which a health plan is established.

## Employer-Sponsored Coverage Can Affect Eligibility

One of the most important restrictions concerns access to subsidized employer health insurance.

Generally, a taxpayer cannot take the self-employed health insurance deduction for premiums attributable to a month during which the taxpayer was eligible to participate in a subsidized health plan maintained by an employer or the employer of a spouse, dependent, or qualifying child under age 27\.

Importantly, **actual enrollment is not the only consideration**. Eligibility to participate in the qualifying subsidized plan can prevent the deduction for that month.

This means freelancers should review employer-sponsored coverage eligibility month by month rather than assuming the deduction automatically applies for the entire year.

## How Business Structure Changes the Rules

### Sole Proprietors and Single-Member LLCs

For a self-employed individual filing Schedule C, the insurance policy can generally be in the name of the business or the individual, provided the applicable requirements for establishing the plan under the business are satisfied.

Keeping premium statements, payment records, and business-income documentation can make the deduction easier to substantiate.

### Partners

Partnership rules require additional coordination.

A qualifying policy may generally be in the partnership's name or the partner's name. Depending on how premiums are paid, the partnership may need to report the amounts appropriately, including as guaranteed payments in applicable circumstances.

Partners should coordinate the treatment of premiums with the partnership's tax preparer rather than assuming that personal payment automatically qualifies.

### More-Than-2% S Corporation Shareholders

S corporation shareholders who own more than 2% of the company face specific reporting requirements.

The policy can generally be in the shareholder's name or the S corporation's name. If the shareholder pays the premiums personally and the corporation is treating them as part of the shareholder's compensation, the corporation generally needs to reimburse the shareholder and report the premiums appropriately as wages on **Form W-2**.

The IRS rules concerning how the plan is established and how premiums are reported are particularly important for S corporation owners.

## Which Insurance Premiums Can Qualify?

The deduction can cover more than a standard individual medical policy.

Qualifying coverage can generally include:

* Medical insurance
* Dental insurance
* Vision insurance
* Certain Medicare premiums
* Qualified long-term care insurance
* Coverage for a spouse
* Coverage for dependents
* Coverage for a child under age 27, even if the child is not a tax dependent

The IRS specifically includes medical, dental, vision, and qualified long-term care insurance within the self-employed health insurance deduction framework.

### Medicare Premiums

Certain Medicare premiums voluntarily paid for qualifying coverage can be included when calculating the deduction.

This can be relevant to self-employed individuals who continue working after becoming eligible for Medicare.

### Qualified Long-Term Care Insurance

Qualified long-term care insurance receives special treatment because the amount of premiums that can be included is subject to annual age-based limits.

For **2026**, the applicable limits per person are:

| Age at End of Tax Year | 2026 Premium Limit |
| ----- | ----- |
| 40 or younger | \$500 |
| 41–50 | \$930 |
| 51–60 | \$1,860 |
| 61–70 | \$4,960 |
| Over 70 | \$6,200 |

The deductible amount for qualified long-term care premiums is generally limited to the lesser of the premiums paid or the applicable age-based limit.

## What the Deduction Does Not Cover

The self-employed health insurance deduction is specifically focused on qualifying **insurance premiums**.

It does not turn ordinary out-of-pocket medical expenses into self-employed health insurance premiums.

Examples include:

* Doctor copayments
* Prescription costs
* Deductibles
* Dental procedures
* Medical procedures
* Other unreimbursed medical expenses

Depending on the circumstances, qualifying unreimbursed medical expenses may instead be considered under the separate itemized medical-expense rules on Schedule A.

The IRS notes that premiums not claimed through the self-employed health insurance deduction may potentially be considered under the itemized medical-expense rules when the applicable requirements are met.

## Form 7206 and Filing Requirements

**Form 7206, Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction**, is used to calculate the deduction in certain situations.

For example, Form 7206 is required when:

* You have more than one source of income subject to self-employment tax
* You file Form 2555 for the foreign earned income exclusion
* You are using qualified long-term care insurance premiums in the calculation

The resulting deduction is generally reported on **Schedule 1, Form 1040, line 17**.

Taxpayers who do not fall into the circumstances requiring Form 7206 may be able to use the applicable worksheet in the Form 1040 instructions instead.

## Marketplace Insurance and Premium Tax Credits

Freelancers purchasing coverage through the Health Insurance Marketplace may also encounter the **Premium Tax Credit (PTC)**.

The interaction between the premium tax credit and the self-employed health insurance deduction can be complicated because the calculation of one can affect the other.

The IRS provides special guidance for taxpayers who may qualify for both benefits.

Rather than treating the insurance premium as automatically deductible at 100%, self-employed taxpayers receiving or potentially qualifying for a premium tax credit should calculate the two benefits together under the applicable IRS rules.

## Health Savings Accounts Are a Separate Tax Strategy

Some self-employed individuals also contribute to a **Health Savings Account (HSA)** when they have an eligible high-deductible health plan.

An HSA contribution can provide a separate federal tax deduction when the applicable requirements are satisfied.

However, the HSA deduction and the self-employed health insurance deduction are separate provisions. The health insurance deduction itself **does not reduce net earnings from self-employment for purposes of self-employment tax**.

This distinction matters when estimating the actual tax savings from health-related deductions.

## Step-by-Step Strategy for Organizing the Deduction

### 1\. Review All Available Coverage

Before purchasing an individual policy, determine whether you or your spouse can participate in an employer-subsidized health plan.

Eligibility can affect the deduction for individual months during the year.

### 2\. Identify Qualifying Premiums

Separate medical, dental, vision, Medicare, and qualified long-term care premiums from ordinary medical expenses.

For long-term care coverage, apply the applicable annual age-based limitation.

### 3\. Match the Policy to the Business

Confirm that the insurance arrangement satisfies the rules for your business structure.

S corporation owners and partners should pay particular attention to reimbursement and reporting requirements.

### 4\. Maintain Detailed Records

Keep:

* Insurance statements
* Premium invoices
* Payment confirmations
* W-2 documentation
* Partnership records
* Business income records
* Marketplace tax forms

Good documentation can make it easier to substantiate the deduction and reconcile the amounts reported on tax forms.

### 5\. Determine Whether Form 7206 Is Required

Review the IRS filing instructions before completing the return.

Multiple self-employment income sources, Form 2555, and qualified long-term care premiums can trigger the requirement to use Form 7206\.

## The Bottom Line

The **self-employed health insurance deduction** can provide meaningful federal income-tax relief for qualifying freelancers, independent contractors, partners, and S corporation shareholders.

The deduction can potentially cover qualifying medical, dental, vision, Medicare, and long-term care insurance premiums for the taxpayer and certain family members. However, the benefit is subject to important restrictions involving business income, employer-sponsored coverage, business structure, and qualified long-term care limits.

For 2026, qualified long-term care premiums have specific age-based limits, while taxpayers with multiple self-employment income sources, Form 2555 filings, or qualified LTC premiums may need to use Form 7206\.

The deduction should also be distinguished from the HSA deduction and from itemized medical-expense deductions. Understanding those differences can help self-employed individuals estimate their actual tax savings and maintain the documentation needed to support their return.
