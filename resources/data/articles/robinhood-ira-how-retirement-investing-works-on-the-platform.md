Retirement investing has traditionally involved choosing between a workplace retirement plan, a traditional IRA, or a Roth IRA and then managing investments through a brokerage platform. Robinhood has expanded that model by offering traditional and Roth IRAs through the same app many investors already use for taxable investing.

The platform offers both self-directed and managed IRA options. Self-directed accounts allow investors to select their own investments, while managed accounts are handled by Robinhood Strategies. Understanding the differences between these account types, along with contribution limits, investment choices, fees, and Robinhood's IRA match, is important before using the platform for long-term retirement savings.

## Robinhood IRA Options

Robinhood currently offers two main types of individual retirement accounts:

* Traditional IRA
* Roth IRA

Each can be opened as either a **self-directed IRA** or a **managed IRA**. Robinhood states that customers can have up to four IRAs on the platform: a self-directed traditional IRA, self-directed Roth IRA, managed traditional IRA, and managed Roth IRA.

The tax treatment depends on the account type.

A traditional IRA generally allows eligible contributions to receive a tax deduction, with taxes generally due when money is withdrawn.

A Roth IRA is funded with after-tax money. Qualified withdrawals can generally be made tax-free, provided the applicable requirements are satisfied.

The investment platform may be the same, but the tax structure surrounding the investments is different.

## How the Robinhood Roth IRA Works

A Robinhood Roth IRA is designed for investors who want to save for retirement while potentially receiving tax-free qualified distributions later.

Contributions are made with after-tax dollars, so contributions themselves generally are not deductible. Investment earnings can grow without annual federal income tax, and qualified distributions can generally be tax-free.

For 2026, the combined contribution limit for traditional and Roth IRAs is **\$7,500**, or **\$8,600 for investors age 50 or older**, assuming the investor has sufficient taxable compensation. This limit applies across all of an individual's traditional and Roth IRAs, not separately to each account.

Direct Roth IRA contributions are also subject to income restrictions. Robinhood lists a 2026 income threshold of \$168,000 for single filers and \$252,000 for married couples filing jointly. The exact eligibility calculation depends on modified adjusted gross income and filing status.

## Traditional IRA on Robinhood

A traditional IRA provides a different tax structure.

Eligible contributions may be deductible depending on factors such as income, filing status, and whether the investor or spouse participates in a workplace retirement plan. Investment growth generally receives tax-deferred treatment, with taxes generally due when distributions are taken.

Traditional IRA withdrawals before age 59½ can potentially be subject to an additional 10% tax unless an exception applies. Required minimum distributions also apply under federal rules.

For investors deciding between a traditional and Roth IRA, the central question is often whether the potential tax benefit is more valuable when money enters the account or when it eventually comes out.

## Self-Directed vs. Managed Robinhood IRA

Robinhood gives investors two ways to manage their retirement portfolio.

### Self-Directed IRA

A self-directed IRA puts investment decisions in the investor's hands.

Robinhood currently allows self-directed IRA investors to purchase stocks and ETFs. Options trading is also available to qualified traders under the platform's applicable requirements. Robinhood Crypto products are not available within its IRA accounts.

This approach may appeal to investors who already have a defined asset-allocation strategy and want to choose their own securities.

### Managed IRA

A managed IRA is handled by Robinhood Strategies.

Instead of selecting individual investments, the investor provides information used to construct and manage a portfolio. This can reduce the amount of day-to-day portfolio management required from the account owner.

Managed IRAs have an important distinction, however: Robinhood states that **managed IRAs are not eligible for its IRA match on contributions**.

That makes the choice between self-directed and managed investing relevant not only to investment control but also to certain platform features.

## Robinhood's IRA Match

One of Robinhood's more distinctive retirement features is its IRA match.

For eligible self-directed IRA contributions, Robinhood currently offers a **1% match without Robinhood Gold**. Gold subscribers can receive a **3% match on eligible annual contributions**. Robinhood Gold currently costs \$5 per month.

For 2026, the annual contribution limit is \$7,500 for investors under 50 and \$8,600 for those 50 or older.

That means the maximum contribution-based match under the current structure would be:

| 2026 IRA contribution | 1% match | 3% Gold match |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| \$7,500 | \$75 | \$225 |
| \$8,600 | \$86 | \$258 |

The match does **not** count toward the investor's annual IRA contribution limit. Robinhood treats the match as interest income earned within the IRA for tax-reporting purposes.

The feature has conditions. Robinhood states that matched funds generally need to remain in the IRA for at least five years to avoid a potential early IRA match removal fee. Gold members also need to maintain the Gold subscription for one year after receiving their first Gold match to keep the full additional Gold match.

The match rate can also change, so investors should verify current terms before making a decision based on it.

## IRA Transfers and 401(k) Rollovers

Robinhood also offers a 1% match on eligible IRA transfers and old 401(k) rollovers into self-directed IRAs, without requiring a Gold subscription. Robinhood states that there is no cap on the match for these transfers or rollovers.

For example, a \$50,000 eligible rollover could generate a \$500 match under the current 1% structure.

This should not be confused with an employer 401(k) match. An employer match is part of an employer-sponsored retirement plan, while Robinhood's match is a platform feature associated with eligible IRA funding.

Investors considering a rollover should also examine investment choices, fees, creditor protections, tax implications, and the features of the existing 401(k) before moving retirement assets.

## Investment Choices

Robinhood's self-directed IRA is relatively straightforward in its investment menu.

Investors can currently use self-directed IRAs to invest in:

* Stocks
* ETFs
* Options for qualified traders

Robinhood's retirement platform also supports recurring investments, allowing investors to schedule contributions and investments at regular intervals.

The narrower investment menu can be an advantage for investors who prefer a simple platform, but it is also something to consider when comparing Robinhood with traditional full-service brokerage firms.

For example, investors looking specifically for a broad selection of individual bonds, certificates of deposit, or mutual funds may need to compare the available choices with other brokerage platforms.

## Building a Portfolio on Robinhood

The account itself does not determine whether a portfolio is diversified.

An investor using a Robinhood Roth IRA could construct a portfolio around broad-market ETFs, individual stocks, or a combination of investments. A managed IRA follows a portfolio constructed through Robinhood Strategies instead.

The key consideration is how the investment selection fits the investor's time horizon and risk tolerance.

Someone with decades until retirement may have a different asset allocation from someone approaching retirement and preparing to use the account for income.

Recurring contributions can also make it easier to maintain a consistent savings schedule. Robinhood currently supports weekly, twice-monthly, monthly, and quarterly recurring IRA contributions.

## Roth Conversions

Robinhood also supports Roth conversions.

A Roth conversion generally involves moving money from a traditional IRA into a Roth IRA. The converted amount may be taxable as ordinary income, depending on the circumstances.

Robinhood currently supports cash-only Roth conversions from self-directed traditional IRAs. The company notes that conversions are generally irrevocable and must be completed by December 31 to count for that tax year.

Because a conversion can create a significant tax liability, investors should consider the effect on their overall taxable income before initiating one.

## Costs and Practical Considerations

The headline attraction of a Robinhood IRA is its integration with a familiar investing platform and its IRA match. But investors should evaluate the entire account rather than focusing on the match alone.

Important considerations include:

* Whether the desired investments are available
* Self-directed versus managed investing
* IRA contribution eligibility
* Roth income restrictions
* Gold subscription costs
* IRA match requirements
* Early match removal provisions
* Investment expenses
* Options availability and risks
* Transfer and rollover considerations
* Customer support and account administration

A 3% match can add money to a retirement account, but it does not eliminate investment risk or guarantee investment returns.

## Robinhood IRA in a Long-Term Retirement Plan

A Robinhood IRA can serve as one component of a broader retirement strategy. The platform offers both traditional and Roth accounts, self-directed and managed investment options, recurring contributions, and an IRA match for eligible self-directed accounts.

The most important decision is still the underlying retirement strategy.

An investor needs to determine how much to save, which tax structure fits the situation, how the portfolio should be allocated, and whether the platform's available investments and services meet those requirements.

For investors who prefer managing their own stocks and ETFs through an app-based platform, the self-directed IRA provides a relatively direct approach. Those who want portfolio management can instead consider the managed option.

The tax advantages of the IRA—not the platform itself—remain the central reason to use a retirement account. Platform features such as the IRA match can add another consideration, but they should be evaluated alongside investment choices, costs, restrictions, and long-term retirement objectives.
